Ask most lifters to describe their program and you'll hear sets, reps, and load. Almost nobody mentions **tempo** - the time each phase of a rep actually takes. Which is strange, because tempo quietly determines how much of your effort turns into adaptation.

## Time under tension isn't the whole story, but it's part of it

The old "time under tension is everything" claim overreached, and the field corrected hard against it. The nuance that survived: *how* you accumulate tension changes the outcome.

A common notation is four digits - for example `3-1-1-0`:

1. **Eccentric** (lowering): 3 seconds
2. **Bottom pause**: 1 second
3. **Concentric** (lifting): 1 second
4. **Top pause**: 0 seconds

## Why deliberately slowing down can help

- **The eccentric is where damage and growth signaling concentrate.** A controlled 3-second lower does more per rep than a dropped one.
- **Pauses kill the stretch-reflex "bounce",** forcing the target muscle to do work the tendons were sneaking away.
- **Slower reps expose weak ranges.** If a squat stalls two inches out of the hole, a paused tempo builds exactly that position.

## Where it backfires

Tempo is a tool, not a virtue. Grinding everything slowly wrecks the *intent* that builds power and top-end strength - the exact opposite of what [velocity work](/blog/velocity-based-training/) is for. Slow the accessory work; keep the main lifts fast and heavy.

The point isn't to lift slowly. It's to stop letting tempo be an accident.
